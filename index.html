Case study · Product design

Enable

A single source of truth for luxury travel.
Constantin Chopin · Founding product designer · 2025–2026
The problem

Every tool owns one vertical. Nobody was trying to bring it together.

  1. Some of them make you productive: itinerary builders, booking systems, the tools that get a trip out of the door.
  2. Some of them hold one vertical of your data and only that one: your clients, your itineraries, your drive, your terms.
  3. Nobody was building the place where all of it meets. So every advisor assembled that picture themselves, by hand, on every single trip.

And this is what that looks like

Not answers. A place that takes PDFs, half-structured spreadsheets and terms buried in email, and returns one record per thing. On the right, one record out of 4.
Surface 3

The morning screen. What changed overnight, what expires soon, and what is waiting on somebody — assembled from the same model, not a separate feed.
Surface 6

The work in progress. Products pulled straight off the directory, so a rate on an itinerary is the same value the record holds rather than a copy of it.
Surface 7

The traveller

Who the work is for. Preferences with a source and a date on each one, and what the product suggests kept separate from what a person confirmed.
Surface 8

The knowledge vault

Where material arrives. A rate sheet forwarded in, a portal synced, an intranet exported — each document keeping its source, its date and who may read it.
The constraint

Two user calls a week. Shipping weekly, sometimes twice.

  1. Design ahead of code could not hold that pace. The first build is what happens when it tries.
  2. So the iteration surface became working software. Four repositories, with the prototype using the design system as a live dependency.
  3. The roadmap held the decisions. The prototype was the specification. There was always something an advisor could use on Thursday.

What came back

The cards had nothing underneath them.

Hotel name. Location. Description. Asked for a Japan itinerary it returned cherry-blossom festivals, cited to Reddit and Wikipedia, when what was needed was the agency's own negotiated terms.
Why the model came before the screens

Retrieval was never the problem. It was three kinds of data pretending to be one.

  • The failure Documents in, answers out, wrong too often to use. Confident answers, wrong often enough to lose an advisor in a week. And when one was right, it was sometimes right about a commission the reader was not allowed to see.
  • What we found Three kinds of data, in the same folders, unlabelled. Canonical, published once and not theirs to edit. Agency — the negotiated terms. Personal — an advisor’s own notes. Nothing said which was which.
  • The move Permission became a property of the record, not a filter on the output. Private by default, opened up deliberately, never the other way round. An answer cannot leak what the model will not join. Their terms are the one thing an advisor has that the desk next door does not.
  • The measure We wrote the target so it could fail. Time to Trusted Answer: sources, freshness, a confidence label or a plain “I don’t know”, and zero permission leakage. Any override is auditable.
